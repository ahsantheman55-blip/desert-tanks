Desert Tanks: North Africa, 1940 to 1943

A tank battle game played by ear. Command a British tank, then a troop and a squadron, from Operation Compass to Tunisia. Later you can switch sides and command a Panzer. The battles are invented, but the tanks, guns, units and history are real. It is about tactics and good shooting, not gore: knocked-out crews always get out safely.

    How to play

    Finding the enemy

    • Each enemy you can see beeps from its direction. A higher beep means closer: about 260 hertz at 3,000 yards, rising to over 1,000 hertz close in. Your chosen target beeps twice.
    • Beeps from behind you are muffled. On the iPhone speaker there is no left and right, so listen to the crew's clock bearings.
    • Clock bearings: 12 o'clock is straight ahead of your hull, 3 o'clock to your right, 9 o'clock to your left, 6 o'clock behind.
    • A soft buzzing beep is an enemy reported by radio that you cannot see yourself. You cannot fire at it until your crew see it.
    • Aircraft give a fast high warble. The crew shout when they come in.
    • Moving tanks raise dust and are seen from further away, and so are you. A tank that has just fired is easy to spot. Hull-down tanks and dug-in guns are hard to see. Sandstorms bring everything close.

    Shooting

    • Choose a target with Next target. The commander names it, its bearing, range, and whether it is facing you, side-on or going away. The gunner traverses, then calls "On!".
    • Fire after "On", and from the halt: firing on the move is very inaccurate. The loader calls "Loaded!" when the next round is ready.
    • The commander watches the fall of shot: "Short, up 200" or "Over, drop 100" means the next round will be closer. Dust and sandstorms can hide the fall of shot.
    • Armour: front is thickest, then side, then rear. Each gun gets through less as range grows. A hit that bounces only shakes the crew. Side-on targets are bigger and usually thinner.
    • The Grant's 75 is low in its hull: turn the tank to face the target to use it. From hull-down only its small turret gun fires.
    • The machine gun is for aircraft and for making anti-tank gun crews keep their heads down.

    Not getting hit

    • Hull-down: the driver finds a fold in the ground so only the turret shows. Much harder to hit.
    • Reverse from hull-down to go turret-down, out of sight, then Hull down again to pop back up somewhere the enemy is not aiming. The enemy's aim is spoilt whenever you move 60 yards or more.
    • Smoke screens you, and them, for about 45 seconds.
    • Face the target to show your thickest armour.
    • Watch fuel, ammunition and the engine. A hot engine in the midday sun can boil over and stop for half a minute.

    Commanding a troop or squadron

    • From level 3 you command a troop of three tanks: you are Able, the others are Baker and Charlie. From level 5, a squadron of three troops.
    • Choose who hears you with Orders to, then give an order: Advance, Hold, Flank left or right, Concentrate fire on your chosen target, or Withdraw.
    • Reports come back by wireless, with a crackle first. They can arrive late and be broken up: Say again asks for a repeat. In radio reports, 12 o'clock is the direction of advance, north, as seen from the tank sending the report. On easy and normal, your operator adds where that is from you.
    • Flanking troops get side shots on tanks that are facing you. That is how to beat thicker armour.

    Keys, for a keyboard

    • Space or Enter on the pad: fire. M: machine gun.
    • Right and left arrows: next and previous target.
    • Up arrow: advance. Down arrow: halt, then reverse. H: hull down. S: smoke. F: face the target. Q and E: turn left and right.
    • W: what's happening. R: repeat. P: pause.
    • Troop orders: T to choose who hears, then 1 advance, 2 hold, 3 flank left, 4 flank right, 5 concentrate fire, 6 withdraw. A: say again.

    With VoiceOver

    • VoiceOver takes over swipes, so the simplest way is the buttons: they all work with VoiceOver. Turn on "Buttons only" to hide the pad.
    • A double-tap on the pad fires. For swipes on the pad, try double-tap and hold, then swipe: VoiceOver passes that gesture through. If the VoiceOver rotor offers Direct Touch here, that works too.
    • Another way is to turn VoiceOver off while you play with the pad, for example with the Accessibility Shortcut, a triple-click of the side button.

    Progress

    • Training and level 1 are always open. Each level opens the next. Passing British level 1 unlocks the German side; then you can switch sides at any time.
    • New tanks unlock as you go: the Grant after level 3, the Crusader Three and Sherman after level 4, the Sandviper after level 6, and an out-of-its-time Firefly after the last level. On the German side: the long-gunned Panzer Three and Four after level 2, the Tiger after level 4 and the Skorpion after level 5.
    • The battle saves itself every 30 seconds and when you leave the page, so a phone call doesn't lose it. Continue it from Choose a mission.
    • Nothing ever costs money.

    History and glossary

    British crew words

    • "Traverse left... steady... on!": the gunner turns the turret until the gun is laid on the target.
    • "Up 200" or "Drop 100": range corrections after watching the fall of shot.
    • Brewed up: a tank set on fire. Bail out: get out of the tank.
    • Hullo Baker, this is Able, over: wireless procedure. Wilco: will comply.
    • Sunray: the commander. Hull-down: only the turret showing over a crest.
    • Honey: the American Stuart light tank. Mark Three Special and Mark Four Special: the British names for the long-gunned Panzers.

    German crew words

    • Panzer, zwei Uhr, achthundert: tank, two o'clock, 800 metres.
    • Erkannt: target seen, gun laid. Geladen: loaded. Feuer: fire.
    • Zu kurz, zu weit: short, over. Treffer: a hit. Abpraller: it bounced. Abschuss: knocked out.
    • Fahrer, marsch: driver, advance. Fahrer, halt. Rückwärts marsch: reverse.
    • Nebel: smoke. Flieger, Fliegeralarm: aircraft. Aussteigen: bail out.
    • Pak: anti-tank gun. Zug: a troop of tanks. Kompanie: a company. Zugführer: troop leader. Chef: company commander.
    • Ghibli: the hot desert wind and sandstorm.

    History notes

    • British tank guns, until mid 1942, were the 2-pounder and then the 6-pounder: good armour-piercing guns that fired no high explosive. German tanks and the 88 could often outrange them.
    • The 88 was an anti-aircraft gun used against tanks. Rommel's tactic was to lure British tanks onto a screen of anti-tank guns, then strike with Panzers.
    • No Panthers ever served in North Africa. The Tiger did, in Tunisia only, from late 1942.
    • Penetration figures in this game are rounded from wartime and post-war trials. They vary a lot between sources and shell types, so take them as a fair guide, not gospel.
    • The Sandviper and the Skorpion are invented. Every battle is invented but set in a real one.
